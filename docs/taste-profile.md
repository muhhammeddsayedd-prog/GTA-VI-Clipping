# Taste profile: what makes a GTA 6 clip win

Built on 18 Nov 2026 (launch day in New Zealand/Asia; Europe and the US unlock tonight) from:
- 117 YouTube Shorts found by searching GTA 6 streamer and funny-moment shorts (views, length, channel size, auto-transcripts)
- 80 top Twitch clips from 20 big streamers (last month and last 24 h)
- Twitch clip data for October–November 2026 published by Klypra and Zoupyu

Limits: I could not watch the videos. This is based on titles, view counts, lengths, channel sizes and auto-transcripts. Views depend heavily on channel size, so compare clips from channels of similar size. Launch-day shorts are only hours old, so their view counts aren't final.

- **Most streamer-reaction shorts above 100k views run 6–16 seconds.** Long "ranking" compilations (45–100 s) mostly reach 25k–190k.
- On Twitch, the most-viewed clips for each streamer this month mostly run 5–22 s (Kai Cenat 12 s/220k, Sodapoppin 5 s/124k, CaseOh 45 s/373k is the exception).
- **Rule: aim for 10–18 s. Allow 6–9 s when the moment is a single reaction. Go above 25 s only for a story with a clear setup and payoff.** Our fixed 30-second cut is about twice as long as what wins.

## 2. Structure: one line, said loudly, early

The winning transcripts are tiny. The payoff is one quotable line, and the clip starts no more than 1–3 s before it:
- "She's so indecisive!" (6.9M)
- "IS THAT A STREAMER?" (1.5M)
- "WAIT, WAS THAT LUCIA?" (98k in a day)
- "I'mma be like 50 years old, bro. I might not even be alive." (40M)
- "SIX STARS. SIX STARS." (Kai, launch week)

The weak launch-day clips ramble, with no single line to remember ("how do we look for way out, bro… it looks so good"). They sit at 200–700 views even from the same streamer.

YouTube's auto-captions write shouted speech in CAPITALS. Nearly every winner has a capitalised burst at its peak. **Loud voice is a real signal, and we currently measure none of it.**

**Rule:** find the loudest, most quotable line (audio peak + transcript + chat peak), start 1–3 s before it, end 1–3 s after the reaction fades. The hook text is usually that line itself or a short summary of it.

## 3. The moments that win (in order of strength)

1. **The streamer finds something about themselves or streamers in the game.** N3on in the trailer, Speed's family business, "they got streamers in GTA 6". Meta moments travel furthest.
2. **Extreme emotion about the game itself:** the countdown hitting zero ("TWO, ONE, LUCIA!"), the delay meltdowns, "SIX STARS". **Launch-night countdowns and unlocks are happening right at the start of streams.**
3. **Firsts during launch week:** first car steal, first police chase, first time wasted, first gun, first ragdoll. Titles follow "xQc's first ___ in GTA 6". Six or more channels posted "xQc steals his first car" within an hour.
4. **A world that reacts or reverses:** an NPC talks back ("xQc gets called a [slur] right after he said so to an NPC", 4.3k Twitch views in hours), cops arriving the second you steal something, a plan failing. A setup followed by a reversal.
5. **Quotable opinions with no gameplay:** "Jynxzi won't marathon GTA 6" (879k, just talking) and Asmongold's take (101k). **Our visual judge would mark these "told, not shown" and reject them.**
6. **Pure gameplay spectacle with no streamer:** the 44M police pursuit, nightclub dancing (16M), Lucia skits. Physics, police and realism details. Our pipeline only finds moments through chat, so it can't find these at all. Consider this as a separate lane later.

**What underperforms:** walkthrough-style story narration, "first look at the graphics" with no reaction, opening-mission cutscenes (also a Rockstar takedown risk), long clips with background music.

## 4. Speed beats polish in launch week

- xQc unlocked at 11:00 UTC. Clips of his first car theft were posted by different channels at 11:26, 11:43, 11:45, 12:17, 12:45 and 13:23. The earliest posters with an existing audience (CLIPPSTA, starzy) got 2–4k views; later ones got a few hundred.
- **Being in the first 30–60 minutes matters more than a perfect edit.** Every step that delays the live lane (the 15-minute warm-up, waiting for a transcript, re-judging) costs views this week.
- Channel size dominates raw views: the same kind of Kai moment gets 6.9M on his official channel and 400 views on a new one. **Judge our clips against our own account median, never against raw views.**

## 5. Rubric for the AI judge (replace the current one)

Score each point from 0 to 2 and add them up (0–14), then map to 1–10:
1. **Hook in the first 2 s:** does the clip open on the reaction or the setup line, not dead air?
2. **One quotable line or one clear visual payoff:** can you title it in under 8 words?
3. **Reaction size:** voice volume and intensity (use audio energy and laughter/scream detection), not only facial expression in still frames.
4. **Reversal or surprise:** expectation vs. outcome, the NPC or cops reacting, being proven wrong.
5. **Meta or launch relevance:** a streamer in the game, a first-time-ever moment, launch or countdown emotion.
6. **Tightness:** no more than 3 s before the setup and no more than 3 s after the payoff. Penalise anything longer than needed.
7. **Understandable without context:** someone scrolling who has never seen this streamer gets it.

Hard rejects stay the same: isolated cutscene or story spoiler, graphic violence against civilians in the first 7 s, safety flags.

Do **not** penalise:
- a talking moment with no visible action, if the line is quotable;
- screaming or laughter with no words;
- in-game radio or score music (that's for the copyright check to decide).

## 6. What this changes in the pipeline

| Finding | Change |
|---|---|
| Winners run 6–16 s | Cut length adapts to the moment: default 12–18 s, minimum 6 s, maximum 25 s for a story. Re-judge at the new length. |
| One loud line is the payoff | Measure audio loudness, and anchor the cut on the loudest point of voice, chat and transcript together. |
| Countdown and unlock reactions happen at stream start | **Remove the 15-minute warm-up now.** Use the channel's stored chat baseline instead. |
| Firsts are the big launch-week format | Add a "first-time" boost: transcript or chat contains "first time", "first ever", or the first occurrence of an event type (car theft, wanted level, death) for that streamer. |
| Quotable talking clips win | Score quotable transcript lines; the visual judge can't reject a clip for having no action on screen. |
| Speed matters most this week | A fast path for strong live spikes: publish within ~30 min and skip waiting for the transcript. |
| Channel size dominates views | Always compare performance against our account's median. |
